01Capital requirements

Fleet I is acquired outright — no vehicle debt — so operating cash flow after launch is unencumbered. Vehicle pricing reflects Tesla's published Model Y pricing with the Full Self-Driving package; California sales tax and fees are included.

Federal clean-vehicle credits sunset on September 30, 2025 and are excluded. Vehicle residual value (modeled at 30% after five years) is additional recoverable capital not credited in the payback figures below — the model is conservative on this point.

02Revenue model

Revenue is rides × fare × uptime. Orange County pairs dense short-hop demand (Irvine, Costa Mesa, Santa Ana) with premium airport corridors (SNA) and event traffic (Disneyland, Honda Center). Benchmarks: established robotaxi operators gross well above these levels per vehicle in comparable metros.

Blend of short in-town hops and higher-value airport runs, ~6.2 paid miles per trip. Priced under equivalent UberX + tip.

Fleet uptime

Vehicles rotate through overnight charging and cleaning; scheduled service is staggered so capacity never drops all at once.

03Operating costs

Everything it takes to run one robotaxi for a month — insurance, staffing, cleaning, charging, parking, maintenance, payments, software, and permits. No driver is the entire point: labor that would be ~70% of a taxi's cost base becomes a shared two-person depot team.

06Risk factors

A candid list. Each of these can move the numbers above, and the conservative scenario exists because of them.

  1. Regulatory timing. Driverless commercial passenger service in California requires DMV and CPUC authorization tied to the vehicle platform. Deployment timing depends on Tesla's permits covering our service area, or interim operation with safety monitors at higher staffing cost.
  2. Platform dependence. The model assumes Tesla robotaxi-spec vehicles and unsupervised FSD perform as marketed. Delays push launch; per-mile remote-supervision fees, if imposed, compress margin.
  3. Insurance market. Autonomous commercial coverage is young. We model $875/vehicle/month; the calculator shows sensitivity up to $1,500.
  4. Utilization ramp. 22 rides/day is a steady-state figure. Months 1–3 will run below it while the service builds ridership; payback in practice shifts a few weeks right of the model.
  5. Competition. Waymo and others may enter Orange County, pressuring fares. The conservative case ($13.50 average fare, 17 rides/day) is our margin-of-safety view.
  6. Vehicle life & residuals. High-mileage duty cycles could steepen depreciation; economic ROI already reserves to a 30% five-year residual.
